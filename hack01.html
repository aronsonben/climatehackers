Boston Climate Action Network (BCAN)

About BCAN

The Boston Climate Action Network (BCAN) advocates for affordable, reliable, and renewable energy for all Boston families.

For many years, BCAN and our allies have led the charge against third-party electric suppliers. These are for-profit corporations that often sign up residential customers with the promise of lower bills, but raise bills later on. Massachusetts families have lost nearly $800M over the past decade to these corporations.

In response to this, BCAN and our allies successfully advocated for the creation of Boston Community Choice Electricity (BCCE), a City of Boston municipal electricity aggregation program. The City purchases electricity in bulk and provides it to residents at a lower rate. The City also purchases more renewable energy than required. In just a few years, BCCE has saved Boston families over $260M and made the City one of the largest renewable energy purchasers in New England.

Current Work

Today, BCAN and our allies advocate to the Massachusetts State House to allow cities to ban third-party suppliers from signing up new residential customers. As part of this campaign, BCAN seeks to illustrate to the public and policymakers how these suppliers harm residents and in what ways.

Read more about BCAN's work at: bostonclimateaction.org

BCAN's Projects

Working on these projects directly helps Boston and MA residents suffering from energy injustices. You can have a direct role in facilitating the end of sketchy energy scam practices by supporting BCAN's initiatives.

Project Name Description Data Sources
Scam Tracker by Neighborhood A GIS map that displays zip-code level data for third-party supplier customer enrollments, along with an estimate of the total financial loss on a ten-year time horizon for that zip-code. MA AGO (2026)
Bill Comparison Calculator Builds off an existing City of Boston tool to help residents understand the difference between the average third-party supplier, BCCE, and the Eversource Basic rate in terms of their monthly/annual average bill. Users enter their current bill amount and selections to get a snapshot of their usage. MA AGO (2026), City of Boston
Third-Party Supplier Complaint Dashboard Modeled after EnergySwitchMA.gov, the state-run website documenting different supply options. It would display two pieces of information for each supplier: the average effective rate (rather than their lower, introductory rate) and the number of complaints filed against them. MA AGO, MA DPU
BCCE Savings Tracker by Neighborhood Similar to the Scam Tracker by Neighborhood, this product would document the zip-code level uptake of BCCE, including by tiers pending data availability. It would track how much each neighborhood has saved with BCCE over a five-year time horizon. City of Boston, MA AGO
Municipal Aggregation Tracker A GIS map of each city/town in Massachusetts, highlighting those with a municipal electricity aggregation program. Users can click a city/town to learn more about its program, including rates and renewable energy standards, and navigate to a page to enroll. MA DPU
BCCE Benefit Tracker A lightweight dashboard detailing the economic and environmental benefits of BCCE, including jobs created, dollars invested, MWs of new solar and wind, and residential cost savings (both aggregated and per-household). City of Boston, MA DPU. External analysis needed.
